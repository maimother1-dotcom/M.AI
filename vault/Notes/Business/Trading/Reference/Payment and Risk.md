# Payment and Risk

As a trader you sit between two parties who do not know each other and are not
taking the risk. You are. This file is how you keep that survivable.

---

## Paying the supplier

**Never full advance to a new supplier.** The normal shape:

| Milestone | Share |
|---|---|
| With the order | 30% |
| Against scanned bill of lading and batch COA | 70% |

Release the balance only after you have seen the shipping documents **and** the
batch COA has passed spec match. Once the balance is paid, your only leverage is
goodwill.

For larger orders or a new supplier, a letter of credit shifts the risk onto
document compliance instead of trust. It costs more and takes longer. Worth it
above a threshold you set and write down here.

---

## Getting paid by the buyer

For a new buyer, advance or a letter of credit. A first-time buyer asking for 60
days credit on a first order is asking you to finance them with money you
borrowed, and it is where new traders lose their working capital.

As the relationship proves itself, extend terms deliberately, in steps, recorded
in the buyer note. Never by drift.

**Credit limit per buyer, written down.** Check it before accepting a new order,
not after.

---

## The exposure that actually kills you

Money out to the supplier before money in from the buyer, on goods that are on the
water and legally yours.

Manage it by:
- Matching milestones where you can. Buyer advance funds supplier advance
- Keeping first orders small enough that a total loss is survivable
- Insuring the cargo. Always. Under CFR you must arrange it yourself
- Never concentrating everything in one buyer or one supplier

---

## Payment redirection fraud

This is routine in this trade, not a rare event.

**Any emailed change of bank details is a fraud attempt until proven otherwise.**

- Verify by voice, on a number you already had. Never a number from the email
  requesting the change
- Suspect look-alike domains. One changed character in a domain name is the whole
  attack
- Suspect urgency: "old account frozen, pay today" is the standard script
- Never send your own bank details in a fresh thread. Reply inside the existing
  one, and expect your buyers to verify yours by phone

Once the money is gone it does not come back.

---

## Other traders' failure modes, worth avoiding

- **Sample material differs from production material.** Which is exactly why the
  batch COA is matched before shipment, every time
- **Supplier goes around you to your buyer.** Strip buyer identity from anything
  sent to China. Neutralise packing lists on direct shipments
- **Buyer goes around you to your supplier.** Do not disclose the manufacturer
  until the relationship justifies it
- **Price validity expires mid-negotiation.** Chinese prices move. Re-confirm
  before accepting a PO against an old quote
- **Currency moves against you** between quote and payment
- **Certificate expires** between order and shipment

---

## Insurance

- **Marine cargo insurance** on every shipment where you carry the risk
- **Product liability**: a raw material that causes a problem in someone's
  finished product is a claim against you as the supplier of record

Both are cheap relative to the exposure. Get them quoted before the first
shipment, not after.

---

## Set these numbers and keep them here

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Minimum margin floor | - |
| Maximum exposure on a single order | - |
| Maximum exposure to a single buyer | - |
| Letter of credit threshold | - |
| Standard payment terms, new buyer | - |
| Standard payment terms, established buyer | - |

Fill these in before the first order. A limit decided in advance is a limit. A
limit decided during a negotiation is a wish.
