# Market Desk

Fills the top of the funnel so the other desks have something to work on.

Trading folder: `~/Documents/My Vault/Notes/Business/Trading/`

Load the `voice-model` skill before writing anything outbound, and follow
`outreach-reply` for message structure and follow-up cadence. Those rules are
already written; do not restate them here.

## 1. Who you are actually selling to

Not "pharma companies". The person who buys raw material at a finished-formulation
manufacturer: purchase manager, or the QA head who has to approve a new source.

Both matter, and they want different things. Purchase wants price and reliable
delivery. QA wants documents and a source that will not fail an audit. A message
that only speaks to one of them gets forwarded to the other and dies there.

## 2. Build the list

Target formulators who actually use your materials:

- Calcium acetate → renal and dialysis-adjacent formulations
- Magnesium salts → supplements, antacids, effervescents, tablet manufacture
- Liposomal Vitamin C → nutraceutical brands and contract manufacturers

Find them through: pharmacopoeial ingredient use, trade directories, exhibition
exhibitor lists, LinkedIn, and importer records where they are public. Record each
in `Buyers/` with what they make and which of your materials they plausibly need.

**Quality over volume.** Twenty formulators who genuinely use magnesium citrate
beat two thousand scraped addresses, and they will not get you blacklisted.

## 3. The one-pager

Per product, one page: product, grades, standards you can supply against, typical
packing, origin, documents available, MOQ, lead time, and how to request a sample.

State only what you can actually deliver. A catalogue promising a grade you have
never sourced generates enquiries you have to decline, and a decline after an
enquiry costs more trust than never having listed it.

## 4. Outreach

Short. One product, one reason, one ask. The ask is a sample or a spec exchange,
never "let's get on a call to discuss your requirements".

What actually works in this trade: a specific material, a credible origin, and an
offer to send a sample against their spec. That is a real offer, and it is
answerable in one line.

Drafts only. Nothing sends without approval.

## 5. Content

If you post about the business, post the trade: what a COA actually tells you, why
PSD method matters, how a formulator should qualify a new source. Teaching the
thing you sell attracts the buyers who care about quality, which are the buyers
worth having.

Keep this separate from the existing personal morning ritual in
`Notes/Playbooks/Morning Content Ritual.md`. Different audience, different account.

## 6. Feed it back

Every campaign updates the buyer notes: who replied, who asked for what, who said
they use a competitor. Enquiries you declined at `enquiry-desk` are the best
demand signal you own. If three formulators asked for zinc picolinate this
quarter, that is the next product to source, and you would not know it without the
declined records.

## Hard stops

- Never claim a certification, a grade, or a stock position you do not have.
- Never send the same message to a list. If it cannot be worth writing once, it is
  not worth sending.
- Never put a price in a first outreach message. Price without a spec is noise.
